Metric — Distance-Weighted Tversky Index

From official metric page:

Let p(x) in [0,1] predicted, g(x) ground truth
k(d) = max(1 - d/R, 0), R=300m = 3px at 100m

TPw = sum_g max_{x:d(x,g)<=R} p(x) k(d(x,g))
FPw = sum_{x:p(x)>0} p(x) [1 - max_g k(d(x,g))]
FNw = sum_g [1 - max_{x:d(x,g)<=R} p(x) k(d(x,g))]

DTI(alpha,beta) = TPw / (TPw + alpha*FPw + beta*FNw + eps)
alpha=0.2, beta=0.8

Worked example: TPw=3.00, FPw=1.89, FNw=2.00 -> DTI=3/(3+0.2*1.89+0.8*2)=0.60
Verified in tests/test_metric.py

Key: every emitted pixel costs 0.2, earns at most 1. So thinning thick surface while keeping geometry raises credit per pixel. Break-even credit per dot ≈0.2*DTI ≈0.052 at 0.26 operating point (derived), 0.0548 measured empirically.

MINE — Why Mutual Information, Not Ablation

Faults cover ~1% area (60,988 catalogue pixels = 1.18% of valid area, hidden ~0.24%). Holdout ablation — add feature, rerun, see if DTI moves — runs on so few positives that delta is noisy, easily mistaken for signal or nothing.

Mutual information directly measures how much feature's value reduces uncertainty about true label, independent of whether any particular model exploits relationship well.

MINE estimator (Belghazi et al., ICML 2018): https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04062
DV representation: I(X;Y)=sup_T E_{p(x,y)}[T] - log E_{p(x)p(y)}[exp(T)]
T is neural network, trained to maximize lower bound. Works for high-dimensional continuous features.

We compute MINE MI between each candidate feature and true label on full label set (not small holdout), use as far-less-noisy first-pass filter — near-zero MI unlikely to earn back submission slot no matter tuning.

Implementation

class MINE(nn.Module):
  net = Sequential(Linear(input_dim,128), ReLU, Linear(128,128), ReLU, Linear(128,1))
  forward(x,y): concat and output T

Training:
  joint: (x,y) same index
  marginal: shuffle y within batch -> p(x)p(y)
  loss = - (mean(T_joint) - log(mean(exp(T_marg))))
  optimizer Adam 1e-3, batch 512, epochs 100

See src/mine_estimator.py

5 Hypotheses — Full Details

1. H38-MT-CONDUCTANCE-EDGE (Rank 1, Expected +0.015 to +0.025, Cost Medium, MI 0.042, PASS 4/4 folds)

  • Layers: GDR 1391 MT Electrical Conductance Maps (5 depth 2-200km, DOI 10.5066/P9TWT2LU) GDR, training_features surface conductivity & depth to conductive base, isostatic gravity anomaly & slope, USGS DeAngelo 2022 conductive heat-flow residual ≥50 mW/m²
  • Physical signature: Sobel/Canny edge detection on MT conductance depth slices (10-20km) to find lateral clay-cap terminations, combined with horizontal gradient of isostatic gravity (gravity worms). Faults = coincident conductance edge + gravity gradient ridge. Transform: Laplacian of conductance, thresholded by gravity worm proximity <300m.
  • Why misses catalogue: USGS QFaults focuses on geomorphic scarps (Holocene). Blind geothermal faults have clay cap (smectite) conductor at 1-3km depth but no surface rupture. MT directly images clay cap; edge indicates fault-controlled fluid pathway termination. Such faults lack topographic scarp, missed by aerial photo, but have MT signature. NBMG reports many hidden systems have MT clay cap without surface fault (Faulds et al. 2021).
  • Differs from prior: Prior used training_features conductivity (surface only) and gravity anomaly, but NOT multi-depth MT conductance maps from GDR 1391 (5 depth slices). Prior H38-1 used heat-flow Euler but not MT edge. H19-4 used geopotential worms but not MT conductance edge. New: depth-slice edge detection targeting clay-cap termination, not just surface conductivity.
  • Source verified: GDR 1391 DOI 10.15121/1881483, MT conductance DOI 10.5066/P9TWT2LU (USGS, public, CC BY 4.0)

2. H38-ASTER-ALTERATION (Rank 2, Expected +0.010 to +0.020, Cost Medium-low, MI 0.031, Registered)

  • Layers: ASTER L1T Band 4 (1.65um), Band 6 (2.205um), Band 8 (2.33um) NASA LP DAAC ASTER, Landsat 8/9 OLI Band 6 SWIR1, Band 7 SWIR2 USGS EarthExplorer EarthExplorer, GDR paleo geothermal sinter/tufa shapefile paleo, detrended elevation slope
  • Signature: Band ratio Al-OH absorption depth = (B4/B6) argillic, (B7/B8) carbonate, (B4+B6)/B5 propyllic. Canny edge + profile curvature >0.01 to find linear alteration halos. Corroborate with paleo sinter density: alteration within 500m sinter gets boost. Linear clay anomaly 100-500m wide along fault-controlled upflow.
  • Why misses: QFaults maps scarps, not mineral alteration. Low-displacement geothermal faults (<10m) have no scarp but extensive alteration halos (10s-100s m) from fluid flow. Alteration persists where fault buried under basin fill. Many hidden systems discovered via alteration mapping (Dixie Valley).
  • Differs: Prior H19-4 used GDR thermal springs/wells geochemistry (water chemistry) but NOT spectral alteration from ASTER/Landsat SWIR. No sibling repo used ASTER band ratios. Prior used DEM openness/LRM (topography) but not SWIR mineralogy. Orthogonal: mineralogical, not topographic/thermal.

3. H38-KNICKPOINT-ALIGNMENT (Rank 3, Expected +0.008 to +0.015, Cost High, MI 0.022, Registered)

  • Layers: 1m DEM tiles from competition CSV 1m_DEM_links.csv (716 tiles, USGS 3DEP 3DEP), detrended elevation & slope, 10m DEM via National Map
  • Signature: D8 flow accumulation threshold 10k cells, chi integral, knickpoint density via slope-break detection (second derivative elevation vs chi >2 sigma). Hough transform to find linear alignments perpendicular to streams (fault crossings). Linear knickpoint alignment + LRM ridge >1m. Transform: planform curvature zero crossing + chi anomaly.
  • Why misses: QFaults relies on range-front scarps, misses subtle faults in basin floors where alluvium buries scarp but drainage records offset. Knickpoints persist 10ka after slip even when scarp eroded. Low-relief basins (Black Rock Desert) have no scarp but drainage deflection.
  • Differs: Prior used DEM openness and LRM (H19-4 L3) but NOT fluvial geomorphology (chi, knickpoint, drainage network). No sibling repo implemented flow routing or Hough on knickpoints. New: fluvial, not hillslope.

4. H38-PALEO-SINTER-LINEAMENT (Rank 4, Expected +0.005 to +0.012, Cost Low, MI 0.018, Registered)

  • Layers: GDR paleo geothermal features sinter/tufa shapefile 82KB, well/spring temperature & chemistry B,Li,SiO2 geothermometer 19.85MB, earthquake density, USGS SGMC geologic map faults
  • Signature: KDE sinter/tufa 500m bandwidth, Hough transform to find linear alignments (fault-controlled paleo discharge). Corroborate B>5ppm or Li>2ppm within 1km. Sinter lineament + geochem anomaly + earthquake density >0.5/km2. Transform: density + Hough + edge of geochem field.
  • Why misses: Paleo sinter indicates past discharge along faults now sealed or no scarp. QFaults maps active Quaternary faults, not paleo hydrothermal features. Many hidden systems have extensive sinter but no mapped fault (Beowawe).
  • Differs: Prior H19-4 L2 used GDR spring/well temps and 2m probe backward inversion, but NOT paleo sinter/tufa lineament. No repo used paleo geothermal features dataset. Prior used geochemistry but not B/Li specifically nor Hough on sinter. New: paleo, not active thermal.

5. H38-INSAR-VELOCITY-GRADIENT (Rank 5, Expected +0.003 to +0.008, Cost High, MI 0.008, Registered)

  • Layers: Sentinel-1 InSAR ARIA standard product JPL ARIA or MintPy velocity MintPy, ASF DAAC Sentinel-1 SLC ASF, training_features dilatation, shear strain rate, second invariant, 1m DEM curvature Laplacian
  • Signature: LOS velocity gradient magnitude via Sobel on InSAR velocity, Laplacian of DEM to find scarps. Faults = coincident velocity gradient ridge + curvature zero-crossing. Curvature transform: DEM Laplacian >0.005 m^-1 + InSAR gradient >2 mm/yr/km. Canny on velocity gradient.
  • Why misses: QFaults misses creeping faults <1mm/yr slip and no Holocene scarp, but InSAR detects interseismic strain accumulation. Blind strike-slip faults in Walker Lane have subtle velocity gradients but no surface rupture.
  • Differs: Prior used training_features geodetic shear/dilation (coarse, Nevada Geodetic Lab) but NOT high-res Sentinel-1 InSAR LOS velocity. No repo used ARIA or MintPy. Prior used DEM openness but not Laplacian + InSAR. New: high-res InSAR, not coarse geodetics.

Holdout Validation — Top Candidate

Four spatially-blocked folds; detector never sees block it is scored on; every rival geometry re-emitted at same pixel count as arm it is compared with, so no contrast won by emitting more mass. Preregistration: GEMSDOE38-PREREG-1.

Promotion rule: mean paired contrast >0 on ≥3 of 4 folds -> PASS

Emission — Why Poisson-Disk + Greedy Coverage

Metric pays dot only when kernel credit clears k > 0.2*s/(1-0.2*s) (0.055 at 0.26 operating point, 0.068 at public leader 0.3195), so emission is coverage problem, not calibration. Expected-marginal-credit greedy dT(x)=Σδ π(x+δ)*max(0,k(δ)-C(x+δ)) and stops at threshold.

On whole-catalogue component holdout (seed 31: 20,870 stand-in hidden pixels, 40,118 known masked), EDGE scored 0.18754 vs best other 0.13848 on proximity field (+0.04906, 4/4 folds), 0.15784 vs 0.14015 on external evidence (+0.01768, 3/4), 0.18368 vs 0.14014 on hybrid (+0.04354, 3/4). Uniform thinning (~0.125), dense quantile (0.060-0.074) and belief-ordered rule (0.031-0.060) beaten decisively.

Our implementation: Poisson-disk 300m (3px) greedy max-coverage sorted by MT edge + gravity + alteration corroboration score.
